## 3. The core flow

**Today—what I do before Kosh:**

1. My colleague passes away suddenly. I worry that my family would not know my insurance details if something happened to me.
2. I search emails, computer files, and paper documents for my policies.
3. I try to understand what each policy covers and how my family would claim it.
4. I collect instructions and contact details from policy documents, brokers, or insurers.
5. I put the information together and tell my family where to find it.
6. I still don’t know whether they could find it and follow the process without me.

**With Kosh:**

1. My colleague passes away suddenly. I decide to prepare insurance information for my family.
2. I open Kosh and add one insurance policy, by uploading the document or entering its details.
3. I review the policy details Kosh shows, correct mistakes, and fill in missing information.
4. I review the claim guide: what my family should do first, whom to contact, which documents they need, and any stated deadlines. Kosh shows the sources and flags anything unconfirmed.
5. I choose the family member who should receive this information and share access.
6. **My family member** opens it and finds the policy, documents, and claim guide.
7. **Job done:** My family member knows where the information is and what to do first. I feel confident they can find it without my help.

**Things it takes to get the job done today:** To confirm from your actual experience.  
**With Kosh:** Five actions after the trigger—add, check details, check guidance, share, and family opens.

**What must not happen:**
- **Step 1:** Kosh must not add to my panic or overwhelm me. I should be able to start with one policy, while seeing clearly that other policies still need preparing.
- **Step 2:** An unreadable, unrelated, or incomplete upload must not become a believable, complete policy. Duplicate, expired, or cancelled policies must not appear as separate, current coverage.
- **Step 3:** Incorrect or missing details must not be treated as confirmed. Kosh must distinguish the policy owner, insured person, and nominee, and let me check extracted details against the original document. Coverage and exclusions must be clear.
- **Step 4:** Kosh must not invent coverage, claim steps, contacts, deadlines, or promise a payout. Guidance must match the situation—death, accident, or hospital treatment—and show its sources. Conflicting or unconfirmed information must remain visible, with a way to check it with the insurer or broker. A document checklist must not imply that those documents are already available.
- **Step 5:** Policy information must not reach the wrong person. I must be able to check who receives access and remove it later. Sharing access must not imply that the recipient is the policy’s recorded nominee.
- **Step 6:** My family must not need my phone or help to access information during an emergency. Saved information must remain available when they return. Opening the page once must not count as proof that they understand it; they should be able to find the policy, claim contact, and first action without me.
- **Step 7:** Kosh must not say my family is fully prepared while policies, documents, or important answers are missing. It must show what is ready, what still needs attention, and when information was last checked, so outdated details do not create false confidence.

**Other flow needed to complete the job:**  
When a death or accident happens, my family opens the shared information, selects the relevant policy, finds the first action, gathers the required documents, and follows the insurer’s claim instructions.

## 4. Onboarding

Who this is for first: Me, preparing insurance information for my family before an emergency.

First value (the moment it first does the job for me): I see a family-readable summary of one real policy: who it covers, the coverage and exclusions, where the policy document is, and what my family should do first for a relevant claim. I can check the details against their sources and see what is missing or unconfirmed. This is the first proof that I can make things easier for my family, not a claim that everything is ready.

The smallest commitment we ask for: One policy document, or the essential details of one policy entered manually. I do not need to gather every policy or invite my family before seeing something useful. If the information is insufficient, Kosh asks only for the missing details needed for this policy and explains why.

The worry it removes (from section 2): "If something happens to me, will my family know where to find my insurance details and what to do first?"

From opening the link to the first value:

1. I open Kosh in my browser after deciding to prepare for my family. I see a short explanation and one main action: "Start with one policy." Removes: "This feels like a big task. Where do I begin?"
2. I upload one policy or choose to enter its details myself. Before uploading, I see a brief, accurate explanation of how my document will be used, kept, and deleted, and that sharing is my choice. Removes: "Do I need all my papers now, and who will see them?"
3. I check the details Kosh reads or the details I entered against the original document, correct mistakes, and answer only essential missing questions. Unreadable information stays marked as missing. Removes: "What if it reads my policy incorrectly?"
4. I see the summary and claim guide my family would read. I can find the first action, claim contact, required documents, and any supported deadlines. Each important detail has a source or is clearly marked for confirmation with the insurer or broker. First value. Removes: "Will this actually help my family, and can I trust the instructions?"

Login: After step 4, when I choose "Save my policy." An account lets me keep private information and return to it. The preview must clearly state whether it has been saved. Before this point, Kosh must explain any temporary document processing honestly; skipping login must not imply that no data is processed or stored.

After the first value:

1. I save the policy and guide to my account. Kosh confirms what was saved and what still needs attention.
2. When I choose to share, Kosh asks who should receive access, shows what they will see, and lets me check the recipient before sharing. I can remove access later. Sharing does not change the policy's recorded nominee.
3. My family member opens the information using their own access, without my phone or assistance. We check that they can find the policy, claim contact, and first action. This is the stronger proof that we are prepared for this policy.

What we don't ask on day one: A long profile, a product tour, every policy, family contact details before sharing, identity documents that are not needed for this task, access to my email or contacts, or details of investments, property, crypto, and wills.

What we ask later, and when:

- Account details when I choose to save after seeing the first value.
- The intended recipient's details when I choose to share.
- Supporting documents when I review the claim checklist; missing documents remain visible and do not block the initial summary.
- Another policy after the first one is useful and saved, at my pace.
- Updated policy details when a policy changes or renews, with the date last checked visible.

The tone throughout: Calm, plain, and specific. Acknowledge the preparation I have actually completed, show what remains, and never promise an approved claim or say my whole family is covered because I added one policy.

## 5\. v1

**Does—the must-haves:**  
I can prepare my insurance information and share it so my family can find the relevant policy and understand what to do first without my help.

- Add policies of any insurance type, including life, health, accident, motor, home, and travel.
- Enter essential details manually: insurer, policy number, insurance type, who or what is covered, coverage dates, recorded nominee where applicable, document location, and claim contact.
- Review and correct a family-readable summary of coverage and exclusions.
- Prepare guidance for the relevant claim situation, showing the first action, required documents, supported deadlines, and sources.
- Clearly flag missing, conflicting, or unconfirmed information. Unsupported guidance must not be presented as complete.
- Preview the family page before creating an account to save.
- Save, update, and share policies with chosen family members; remove access when needed.
- Let family members access the information without my phone or assistance.

**Doesn’t—not this sprint:**  
Investments, mutual funds, deposits, property ownership records, crypto, and wills; automatic email scanning; submitting claims; tracking payouts; an open-ended AI assistant; guarantees of coverage or claim approval.

**Nice to have—only after the must-haves work:**  
Automatically reading uploaded documents, downloadable reference copies, and reminders to review or renew policies.

**How I’ll know it worked:**  
I prepare and share real policies. My family member can identify the relevant policy, find its claim contact, and explain the first action without my help. Later, they can find the information again, and I return to add another policy or update existing details.

## 6\. The riskiest guess

**If this is false, the product is pointless:**  
Many immediate family members do not know where the insurance details are or how to claim. I have seen this in 7 out of my 7 family members.

**Thirty-minute check, without code:**  
Speak individually with three immediate family members. Before showing them Kosh or explaining the idea, ask them to demonstrate:

1. Where they would find an existing family insurance policy.
2. How they would identify whether it is relevant to a particular situation.
3. Where they would find the claim contact and first instructions.

Use an actual policy and a situation relevant to it. Let them use the documents and tools they normally rely on, without prompting them.

**What would challenge the guess:**  
If all three can find the relevant policy and dependable claim instructions without help, the assumption is weaker for this group. If they know where policies are but cannot find claim instructions, the main problem is guidance rather than storage.

**What happened so far:**  
My experience with seven family members suggests this problem exists. The structured check above has not yet been run.

**What changes after the check:**  
Record exactly where people struggle. If finding policies is the problem, prioritise organisation and access. If understanding claims is the problem, prioritise clear, sourced guidance. If neither is a problem, reconsider who needs Kosh before expanding the build.
